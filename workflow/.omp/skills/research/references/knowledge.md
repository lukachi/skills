# Research Knowledge Management

- Research holds topic knowledge, never the state of our code. Reports and notes describe external practice: other products, engines, tools and libraries, their capabilities, limits, licences, options and trade-offs. They never state what our repositories implement, lack or break, never cite our files, modules, symbols or line numbers, and never record our version pins or test results. The state of our code belongs in specs and change artifacts.
- Preserve accepted worker reports in `research/inbox` without rewriting their findings.
- Maintain reusable topic notes in `research/knowledge`.
- Each topic note links to its source reports and records the scope, conclusions, limitations, and research dates.
- Before commissioning research, consult relevant existing knowledge and identify actual gaps.
- Reuse applicable findings instead of repeating research.
- When evidence is outdated, incomplete, or contradictory, commission focused follow-up research.
- Never silently overwrite conflicting findings.
- Preserve the provenance of every reused conclusion.

## Knowledge Updates

- Before creating a knowledge note, search for existing notes covering the same topic.
- Update relevant existing notes instead of creating duplicates.
- A knowledge note is a navigational index, not a replacement for original research.
- Link findings to their specific source reports in `research/inbox`.
- When new research changes previous conclusions, preserve both the old and new findings with their provenance.
- Explicitly mark superseded, conflicting, or outdated findings. Never silently erase them.
- Preserve relevant details through links to original reports rather than duplicating their contents.
- Do not repeat investigations merely to update a knowledge note.

## Knowledge Note Format

When creating or updating a topic note, follow
[Knowledge Note Format](knowledge-note.md).

Knowledge notes are navigational indexes.
Original research reports remain the source of evidence.

- Do not create a knowledge note for every research report.
- Create or update knowledge notes only when findings
  contribute to a reusable topic.
- A single report may contribute to multiple topic notes.
- A single topic note may reference multiple reports.
- Reports without reusable findings remain in the inbox;
  no knowledge note is required.
