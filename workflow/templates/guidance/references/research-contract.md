# Project-context research contract

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the closest primary authority available:

1. binding law, regulation, standard, protocol, or specification;
2. official product or platform documentation and release notes;
3. original research, source repository, dataset, or maintainer statement;
4. strong secondary analysis used with explicit attribution;
5. informal discussion used only as a lead or perspective.

Popularity, search rank, repetition, and polished prose do not increase
authority.

## Claim matrix

For every material conclusion record:

| Claim | Source | Source class | Version/date | Supports or conflicts | Applicability | Confidence limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Split mixed conclusions into atomic claims. A source may support one claim and
be irrelevant to another.

## Freshness and scope

State when the answer can drift. Check the current official version for
software, standards, laws, prices, policies, and platform behavior. Preserve
the exact scope: jurisdiction, product tier, runtime, protocol version,
population, or experiment conditions.

## Trust boundary

The research packet is an operational candidate. Search tools and the agent's
synthesis are navigation and interpretation, not authority. Promotion requires
the normal workflow gate:

- primary source for external facts;
- maintainer authority for project intent and normative choices;
- pinned source/runtime evidence for implementation;
- semantic verification for the authored knowledge view.
