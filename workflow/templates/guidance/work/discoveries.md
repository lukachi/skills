# What to preserve, and where

Before continuing, apply this test:

> If this newly learned information disappeared, could a fresh session repeat
> material investigation, choose differently, misunderstand the work, or act
> unsafely?

If yes, append a complete entry to the owning record's discovery ledger: the
observation and its uncertainty, direct evidence or missing evidence, the
implication, the applicable scope or lifetime, and the current disposition.

The trigger is **consequence of information loss**, not a fixed taxonomy. It is
not "traps" or "findings" or "lessons" — those categories are what make an agent
skip a fact that fits none of them.

Where it goes: the claimed unit during execution, the parent record during
shaping and final review, or a linked artifact when the supporting material is
too large — in which case the ledger must link it and keep ownership explicit.
If the discovery changes parent scope, criteria, or decisions, update the parent
as well.

Preserve invalidated discoveries with a corrected disposition rather than erasing
them. The lineage is what stops the same wrong conclusion being reached twice.

Routine activity is not a discovery. The checkpoint may reference an entry and
its effect on the next action, but does not duplicate it.

Small jobs noticed in passing are neither discoveries nor blockers. They go in
the checkpoint's own list, which survives a checkpoint that says nothing about
them and reaches the next session. Anything you intend to "come back to" and
leave only in prose is lost with the context holding it.
